# Original Vs Final Patterns

Use this reference when the user provides:

- a rough original draft
- a final approved version
- or a pair of "before / after" texts from the same piece

This file captures what changed between one source draft and one accepted final version of the user's first featured article copy.

## Why This Matters

The difference between the rough draft and the approved final draft shows the user's editing taste more clearly than either draft alone.

In this case, the user consistently moved toward:

- shorter emotional arcs
- tighter paragraph purpose
- fewer stacked anxieties
- less explicit persuasion
- more grounded action language

## Stable Editing Patterns Revealed By This Pair

### 1. Replace broad emotional accumulation with one clearer line of pressure

The original draft listed multiple anxiety dimensions in detail:

- future life
- career development
- health
- time
- intimate relationships

The final version kept the same underlying idea but compressed it into:

- uncertainty about life, work, and growth
- AI amplifying that uncertainty

Implication:

- when the point is already clear, compress the list
- keep the emotional truth, remove surplus enumeration

### 2. Reduce abstract philosophical layering if a simpler sentence can hold the point

The original version explained the deeper anxiety in a more reflective way.
The final version kept the same meaning but made it easier to say aloud and easier to place on pages.

Implication:

- choose a sentence that can be spoken directly
- prefer one clean layer of explanation over multiple nested reflections

### 3. Keep the trigger and concept, but simplify the emotional commentary around them

The original draft framed the video and its ideas with more buildup.
The final version moved more directly to:

- the video
- the concept of "AI 时代的重新教育"
- the two main takeaways

Implication:

- when a trigger event is credible on its own, do not over-describe how powerful it felt
- let the idea carry the weight

### 4. Convert "learning AI" from a large worldview statement into a usable behavioral distinction

The rough draft contrasted:

- learning a few tools
- fragmented watching
- collecting prompts
- false sense of understanding

The final version retained this idea but sharpened it into:

- staying at the level of "了解和观望"
- needing to "真正用起来"
- using AI to experience its capability boundary

Implication:

- the user likes contrasts that lead to behavior
- "观望 vs 用起来" is stronger than longer diagnostic explanation

### 5. Pull the ending back from invitation or mobilization

The original ending included:

- inviting similarly anxious people to relate
- encouraging them to act
- calling on them to truly use AI

The final version still had one short comment invitation, but the main close returned to:

- recording the process
- continuing to learn
- improving with AI

Implication:

- keep the close centered on the user's own practice
- avoid turning the ending into a motivational speech unless the user explicitly wants that

## Reusable Heuristics

- Compress emotional lists unless each item adds a genuinely new layer.
- If two sentences make the same point, keep the one that is easier to say aloud.
- When a concept already sounds strong, remove extra "this impacted me deeply" scaffolding.
- Prefer action distinctions over theory summaries.
- Keep the ending close to the user's own next step.

## What To Preserve From The Rough Draft

Even though the final version is tighter, the rough draft still reveals durable preferences:

- AI anxiety is connected to life-stage anxiety
- learning with AI should not stay at the tool-list level
- the user's account is about recording growth, not performing expertise

These ideas should continue to appear in future drafts, even if expressed more simply.

## What To Avoid Reintroducing

- long lists of anxiety categories
- repeated restatement of the same fear
- audience-directed motivational wrapping at the end
- stacked abstract sentences when one practical sentence will do
