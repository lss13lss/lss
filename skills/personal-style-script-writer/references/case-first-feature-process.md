# Case: First Featured Video Optimization Process

Use this reference when the user is writing:

- account introduction content
- a first featured post or pinned video
- "why I started this account"
- personal positioning content tied to AI, learning, growth, or self-observation

This file captures one strong working example of the user's current preferences.

## What This Case Reveals

The user does not want a generic "AI content creator" opening. The preferred center of gravity is:

- a real personal starting point
- emotional truth before conclusions
- a clear trigger that changed the user's framing
- a method-level stance, not just a list of tools
- a restrained and believable closing

## Preferred Narrative Logic

For this kind of content, prioritize this progression:

1. Start with a sentence that creates attention.
2. Move quickly into the user's anxiety, confusion, or pressure.
3. Show that the surface anxiety is not the whole story.
4. Introduce the trigger event, video, conversation, or realization.
5. Extract the deeper idea from that trigger.
6. Turn that idea into the purpose of the account.
7. End by inviting people into an ongoing real process rather than announcing a finished identity.

## Strong Signals From The User's Revision Feedback

### 1. The content should center on motive, not achievements

The user explicitly narrowed the first featured content to one question:

- why this account exists

Avoid drifting into:

- feature lists
- channel columns
- experiment summaries
- results recap

### 2. The origin needs a real trigger

The user's preferred version became stronger after introducing a specific video and its ideas. That means:

- do not present the account decision as abstract inspiration
- anchor it in a real encounter, stimulus, or moment of reframing

### 3. The emotional layer matters

The user added a structured anxiety model:

- surface: action stuck, procrastination, not starting
- middle: uncertainty about future life and work
- deep: lack of control, no convincing long-term picture

This implies the draft should not flatten everything into "AI replacement anxiety."

### 4. The real differentiation is methodological

The account should not sound like:

- "I share the latest AI tools"
- "I summarize AI news"

It should sound closer to:

- don't only watch what others do with AI
- talk to AI yourself
- learn systematically
- build your own collaboration method

### 5. The user's voice rejects heavy manifesto language

The user repeatedly preferred wording closer to:

- record and supervise my growth
- relearn
- understand myself again
- really treat AI as a partner

The user rejected stronger slogan-like wording such as:

- complete growth loop
- self-reinvention manifesto
- disruptive destiny language

## Writing Heuristics For Similar Drafts

- Start from lived experience, not account strategy.
- Use emotional progression such as "first excited, then anxious" when supported by the user's notes.
- Let the deeper layer arrive gradually instead of explaining everything in the first paragraph.
- Keep the turning point concrete.
- Convert the turning point into a belief about how to live or learn in the AI era.
- Make the account a vehicle for that belief, not the star of the piece.
- Keep the ending open and human. The right ending often sounds like "this is a real process I want to record" rather than "this is the mission of my brand."

## Reusable Structure For This User

1. Attention line
2. Emotional state and pressure
3. Deeper explanation of the anxiety
4. Trigger event
5. New understanding
6. Method stance
7. Account purpose
8. Audience connection

## Language To Prefer

- spoken wording
- natural transitions
- self-observation
- believable uncertainty
- first-person perspective

## Language To Avoid

- empty inspiration phrases
- overly complete concluding summaries
- polished but generic success framing
- dramatic self-branding
- obvious AI-writing cadences
